NanoBrasil and UFLA join forces to accelerate innovation in Brazilian poultry farming

Jul 16, 2026·NanoBrasil News Agency·2 min read

The union of two national references in their fields promises to drive a new generation of technologies for Brazilian poultry farming.

On one side, NanoBrasil, the largest nanotechnology hub in Latin America. On the other, the Federal University of Lavras (UFLA), an institution internationally recognized for its agricultural sciences research and home to the Poultry Technology Research Center, where professor Antônio Gilberto Bertechini, one of Brazil's foremost authorities on poultry farming, works.

The partnership brings together scientific knowledge, research infrastructure and technological development to validate nanotechnology-based solutions for poultry litter management, one of the main health and production challenges in the broiler chain. The first tests were completed after a full rearing cycle, monitored by UFLA researchers and NanoBrasil's technical team.

“Professor Bertechini is very excited about how the work is going. Now we will have all the data in hand to evaluate the results and take the next steps. This partnership expands our capacity to develop technologies that bring more productivity, better animal welfare and more efficiency to Brazilian poultry farming,” says Oscar Geigner, co-founder and commercial director of NanoBrasil.

Science applied to the field

The trials were conducted at UFLA's Poultry Technology Research Center over 42 days, the period corresponding to a full broiler production cycle.

During this period, researchers monitored indicators related to animal performance and poultry litter conditions, including weight gain, the occurrence of pododermatitis and various sanitary parameters.

The partnership was important for understanding how nanotechnology can influence both the production cycle and the sanitary cycle of poultry litter.

“We will now move on to new tests, evaluating different production cycles and how this technology holds up throughout production,” says Manuel Rodríguez, NanoBrasil's Innovation and Development coordinator.

Next stage: validation at scale

With the initial results considered promising, the expectation is to move on to new validation phases, bringing the technology closer to the conditions found on commercial farms.

“Knowing the good results this tends to generate, we are going to start tests at industrial scale. The idea is to offer healthier ways of rearing, reduce stress on the birds, shorten the downtime between flocks and contribute to more efficient production,” says Oscar Geigner, before concluding:

“We have also already discussed new applications, such as using biocidal paints in poultry houses to further strengthen sanitary control.”

Competitiveness for the world's largest exporter

The partnership between NanoBrasil and UFLA strengthens a line of research focused on one of the main bottlenecks in Brazilian poultry farming. In a sector that exports almost US$ 10 billion a year, efficiency gains in poultry litter can mean fewer health losses, shorter downtime between flocks, better production performance and preservation of the commercial value of a market worth billions of reais.

This combination of nanotechnology, applied research and validation at one of the country's leading universities reinforces the initiative's potential to contribute to the competitiveness of Brazilian poultry farming.
