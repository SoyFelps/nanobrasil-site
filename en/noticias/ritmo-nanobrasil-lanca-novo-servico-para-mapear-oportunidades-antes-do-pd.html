NanoBrasil creates a technology X-ray to reveal market opportunities

Aug 7, 2026·NanoBrasil News Agency·5 min read

Investing months in Research & Development (R&D) does not guarantee that a company will arrive at a new product. The solution may already exist, be protected by third parties or simply find no room in the market. To tackle one of the main challenges of the innovation process, which is making decisions with little information in the early stages of development, NanoBrasil is launching the Technological, Strategic and Market Intelligence Report, which brings together scientific, patent and market analyses even before the first laboratory tests.

The analysis covers scientific articles, patents filed in different countries, technology trends, competitors, potential partners and market indicators. From this X-ray, the team identifies up to ten promising technologies, assesses risks and opportunities, and digs deeper into the routes with the greatest technical, economic and commercial viability.

“The main gain is not starting in the dark. The report shows what has already been developed, which paths are protected and whether there is a market for the solution. In some cases, the decision will be to move forward. In others, it will be to change course before losing time and money,” says Oscar Geigner, co-founder and CCO of NanoBrasil.

Before the bench

The work begins with a prior-art study, a process that investigates the scientific and technological knowledge already available about a given solution. The company presents a need, a production difficulty or a possible new product. The NanoBrasil team turns this challenge into a specific search strategy.

The searches reach scientific databases and international patent databases, including ScienceDirect, Scopus, Lens, Espacenet and the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). Academic repositories and the database of Brazil's National Institute of Industrial Property (INPI) may also be consulted.

This is not about typing keywords and collecting documents. The team builds its own search equations for each project, filters the results, analyzes the relevant studies and organizes an exclusive database on the problem presented by the client.

“We create a search capable of narrowing results to the technology that really matters. Then we read the documents, analyze the data and turn this volume of information into trends, risks and opportunities for the company,” explains Manuel Rodríguez, NanoBrasil's Innovation and Development coordinator.

A map in data

One of the studies that served as the basis for the new product resulted in a 37-page document with 13 figures on nanoparticles applied to repellent fabrics. The analysis brought together scientific publications, patents, recurring technologies, filing countries, companies active in the sector and possible restrictions on freedom to operate.

The charts showed the evolution of research and patent filings over two decades. The report also identified the most recurrent technologies, keyword clusters, international patent classes and the companies leading development in that market.

In the case analyzed, the United States accounted for 109 records and Europe for 33. Brazil had only four national patents directly related to the assets researched, but appeared as the destination of 73 international filings extending protection to the country. The contrast pointed to a relevant market for foreign companies, though with little local generation of intellectual property.

The survey also flagged potentially restrictive patents and indicated alternative technical paths. This reading reduces the risk of a company moving forward on a route that may later require licensing, royalty payments or reformulation.

Ten possible routes

The new format expands the technology scouting NanoBrasil was already doing. After mapping the accumulated knowledge and the intellectual property landscape, the team selects up to ten technologies with the greatest potential to generate new products.

Each alternative undergoes a strategic analysis. The assessment considers technology maturity, technical limitations, the presence of competitors, market size, the production chain, intellectual property risks and the conditions needed to reach industrial scale.

The company receives a presentation of the results and takes part in selecting one or two routes best aligned with its structure, its goals and its expected time to market.

“The company doesn't receive a pile of articles and patents. It receives a reading of what these documents mean for the business and which technologies offer the best conditions to move forward,” highlights Manuel.

In the next stage, the analysis focuses on the chosen alternatives. The report then details regulatory barriers, laboratory validation needs, production capacity, scale-up possibilities and the steps needed to turn the opportunity into a product.

The market counts

Prior-art studies and technology scouting are already used in innovation environments. NanoBrasil's differentiator is the presence of strategic and market intelligence throughout the entire process.

A technology can be scientifically promising and still face a small market, an unviable supply chain or competitors with broad intellectual protection. It can also reveal the opposite: an underexplored area with growing demand and room for national development.

The mapping makes it possible to identify who may compete in the market, who dominates a given technology and which organizations could become partners, suppliers or licensors. A patent found during the search does not necessarily mean the end of the project. It can lead to a new formulation, an alternative route or a business partnership.

“It's not enough to know whether someone has already done it. You need to understand whether it's commercially worthwhile, who is in that market and which path will lead the company to a competitive solution. This intelligence changes the quality of the decision,” says Oscar.

R&D ground zero

The report can be contracted as a standalone deliverable. The company receives the study, the base of documents analyzed, the charts, the strategic assessment and the recommendations prepared by the technical team.

When there is interest in continuing, it becomes the starting point of a project led by NanoBrasil. The final deliverable includes the proposed development scope, the technical stages, the resources needed and the route defined to take the solution to the bench and, later, to the market.

Initial contacts with large companies have already resulted in contracts and new negotiations. The interest reflects a growing demand for innovation decisions supported simultaneously by science, intellectual property and commercial intelligence.

“Nanotechnology still seems distant to many executives and technical teams. The report turns this universe into concrete choices, shows where the opportunities are and makes clear how NanoBrasil can lead the development,” says Oscar.

After the results are presented, the company chooses the technology it wants to prioritize. NanoBrasil then delivers the follow-up proposal with the technical path, the development stages and the strategy to turn the identified opportunity into a new product.
