When small lesions cost billions: the invisible impact of poultry litter

Jul 14, 2026·NanoBrasil News Agency·3 min read

Small gains in poultry litter management can have significant impacts on productivity, biosecurity and even farm profitability. Far beyond the birds' comfort, the quality of this environment influences indicators such as feed conversion, mortality, condemnations, downtime between flocks and the commercial value of cuts destined for international markets.

In a sector that produces about 6.46 billion chickens a year, according to IBGE, and keeps Brazil as the world's largest exporter of chicken meat, every operational improvement is an opportunity to increase efficiency and preserve the chain's competitiveness.

Far beyond comfort

Although often seen merely as the material covering the floor of poultry houses, litter plays a central role throughout the rearing cycle.

It needs to absorb moisture, provide comfort to the birds and contribute to a sanitarily balanced environment. When these conditions are no longer met, problems begin to arise that affect both the animals and the economic performance of production.

“Poultry litter works as an insulator and its function is to absorb moisture and provide comfort to the birds. Its quality is directly related to animal welfare, because it needs to maintain low moisture levels. When that doesn't happen, the material mixes with excreta, ferments and encourages problems, especially pododermatitis. That's why good-quality litter is essential to ensure good performance in broiler production,” explains professor Antônio Gilberto Bertechini, one of Brazil's foremost references in poultry farming.

When a detail affects the whole operation

Litter with excess moisture encourages the proliferation of microorganisms, increases ammonia emissions and creates conditions for the development of the lesser mealworm (Alphitobius diaperinus), an important disease vector in poultry farming.

The impacts go beyond animal health. Lesions such as pododermatitis compromise the birds' welfare and can reduce the commercial value of the feet: a product that, in international markets, can be worth about US$ 3,800 per ton, even surpassing chicken breast, sold at around US$ 2,800 per ton.

According to an estimate presented by Bertechini, approximately 35% of birds develop some kind of lesion that prevents the feet from being sold, indicating potential losses of billions of reais for the production chain.

In addition, the presence of pathogens may require more intensive cleaning and disinfection between flocks, extending downtime and reducing farms' production capacity.

Financial return starts with biosecurity

For Oscar Geigner, co-founder and commercial director of NanoBrasil, investing in poultry litter quality should be seen as an operational efficiency strategy.

“When we talk about poultry litter, we're not just discussing one item of management. We're talking about a factor that influences productivity, animal welfare, biosecurity and competitiveness. The fewer health losses there are, the greater the potential return for the entire operation.”

This logic was precisely the starting point for developing the platform created by NanoBrasil.

“We began to study the poultry chain in depth and understood that the biggest challenge was not just controlling microorganisms, but the entire poultry litter environment. It was precisely this complexity that caught our attention. We realized there was an opportunity to develop a technology that would act on several of these problems at the same time,” says Manuel Rodríguez, the company's Innovation and Development coordinator.

Efficiency on several fronts

The platform developed by NanoBrasil uses nanotechnology to act simultaneously on the control of moisture, ammonia emissions, microbiological load and the lesser mealworm.

Instead of treating each challenge in isolation, the idea is to act on the different variables that determine litter quality throughout the production cycle.

This approach seeks to help reduce economic losses, preserve the commercial value of production, lower health risks and increase farms' operational efficiency.

In a market where competitiveness is built on details, poultry litter quality is no longer just a management issue and becomes a strategic investment for the entire poultry chain.
